# One-clinic controlled pilot — 1–2 weeks

Purpose: determine whether the certified candidate is safe and usable in the
clinic's actual workflow, and resolve operational limits before wider use.
This plan does not authorize production deployment or company-wide rollout.

## Start conditions and owners

Leadership must approve the limited scope and stop criteria. Security/privacy
must explicitly permit browser-local patient data. IT must accept managed,
encrypted devices and the profile, storage, retention, replacement and recovery
policies. Clinical operations must accept workflow scope, authorized local
signing/addenda and the Tebra filing/verification SOP.

Name one operational pilot owner, one IT/support owner and a clinical lead.
Include representative MAs/front-office staff and at least one provider. Record
the actual Windows/Edge builds, browser profiles, screen configurations and
physical printers. Keep the exact reviewed package and its checksums available.
Begin with synthetic cases; use patient data only after the prerequisites above
are written and accepted.

## Schedule

| Time | Work | Evidence / exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before day 1 | Admin/IT/clinical review; synthetic demo; device/printer acceptance | Written decisions, named owners and approved fallback |
| Days 1–2 | Train staff; rehearse all four workflows, optional disclosures, resume/recovery, failed save, local signing, copy/print and Tebra filing | Staff identify patient, required work, save/lock state and filing responsibility without coaching after training |
| Days 3–5 | Limited one-clinic use within approved scope; daily owner review | Safety/filing checklist for each case, incident log and staff observations |
| Week 2, if approved | Continue representative cases/staff within the same clinic; resolve early issues and repeat affected checks | Managed-workstation/printer evidence and reconciled incidents |
| End of week 1 or 2 | Clinical, operations and IT retrospective | Stop, extend/remediate, or request a limited multi-office pilot |

Do not include TMS, letter authoring or assumed synchronization/scheduling in the
pilot. Routine work should not require opening unused optional sections.

## Daily measures

Track case counts by workflow, supported screen/profile/printer, task time or
interaction observations, save/sign/print failures, recovery outcomes, support
incidents and confirmed Tebra filing. Use privacy-approved identifiers and
reporting systems. Do not put patient data into repository issues or public demos.

For every case, staff must know which patient is active, what remains required,
whether work is saved or signed locally, and who confirmed filing in Tebra.
Verify printed patient identity, required treatment content and all pages.

## Safety stop criteria

Pause the affected pilot workflow and notify the named owners if there is any
patient crossover, wrong-patient AVS, silent draft loss, dishonest saved/signed
state, hidden required blocker, required treatment missing from output, or false
implication that local copy/sign is Tebra filing. Investigate a supported-use
storage loss or print failure before resuming that configuration.

Use the clinic's existing approved documentation workflow as the fallback.
Preserve the current encounter and approved incident evidence; do not clear
browser storage, delete records or improvise a migration as a repair. Reconcile
final documentation with the chart of record under the clinic's SOP.

## Success and exit

Require zero critical safety failures above; verified Tebra filing; successful
real-printer output; no unexplained supported-use storage loss; understood
fallback/recovery limits; and tracked, reconciled support incidents. Staff should
explain patient identity, required versus optional work, disclosure behavior and
local save/sign versus external filing without coaching after training.

Rehearse rollback before broader use. Returning to the existing approved clinic
workflow must preserve the original browser profile/storage and final chart
documentation. Different origins/profiles/files do not automatically share local
records. The pilot owner and IT must document who authorizes rollback and how
remaining cases are reconciled.

At the retrospective, record the evidence and decision. A limited multi-office
pilot requires a separate approval and representative-site validation.
Company-wide deployment requires another explicit leadership decision after
pilot issues, IT/security, training, support and configuration questions are closed.
