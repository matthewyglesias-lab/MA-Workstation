# Workstation clinical policy adapter

Version `2026-09-15.1`. Reviewed against the primary prescribing information below on 2026-09-15. This is a console policy overlay around the preserved MA-Workstation engine, not a change to the vendored source and not clinical authorization. Tebra's verified order, history, and prescriber direction remain authoritative.

## Integration contract

`evaluateWorkstationClinical(encounter, context)` invokes the original engine, preserves its product, preparation, identity, vitals, acute-safety, and final-review checks, and overlays the narrow corrections below. It returns the same `ClinicalEvaluation<InjectionEvaluationOutput>` shape. `workstationSchedule` and `workstationNextDate` provide corrected staff-facing calculations; consumers must use these instead of the vendored catalog's historical week approximations.

Context includes the verified indication, previous product and dose, and `priorMaintenanceDoses`: the number of earlier treatment injections, not the console's physical-component `doseSequence`. The latter is explicitly ignored. Unknown treatment stage or previous strength produces a history-review prompt; no tier is guessed. A documented different or unrecognized previous formulation suppresses same-product missed-dose evaluation and requires transition-plan review.

`workstationPolicyReviewFingerprint` binds a provider review to policy version, original timing facts, indication, previous product/dose, verified previous injection count, and verified plan text. Only provider-authorized review with provider, time, instructions, and this exact fingerprint satisfies a reviewable finding. A generic acknowledgment does not. Findings requiring re-initiation additionally require an initiation/re-initiation/loading phase and an explicitly verified plan. A nonstandard interval or indication can instead retain its actual phase with an explicit provider-directed plan. Hard date/site/needle-input findings remain blocking in the prospective path.

`workstationDocumentationEncounter` returns a local clone whose current policy fingerprint is translated for the original note adapter. It translates an already-current whole-encounter final review consistently; it never repairs a stale review or mutates the saved receipt. Retrospective facts and stock reconciliation are the surrounding server workflow's responsibility; this evaluator does not turn a provider note into evidence that prior review or administration occurred.

Site-dependent needle resolutions for paliperidone products and body-type-dependent aripiprazole resolutions cannot silently choose a branch when weight or habitus is missing. The original sourced needle table remains unchanged; the console requires the missing input for prospective review. Site/dose-specific supplied-kit requirements and existing Vivitrol body-habitus safeguards remain present.

The four-week selections for the existing monthly workstation pathways remain explicit four-week order comparisons; they are not relabeled as calendar-month computations. A selected interval outside a product's referenced routine options gets no calculated date or generic timing window. Other/custom products and provider-directed restart paths remain non-calculating.

## Verification and limits

Focused tests cover month-end, leap year, calendar-window edges, Sustenna first-maintenance anchoring, product/strength/stage-specific missed-dose boundaries, unknown indication/history, changed formulations, nonstandard intervals, preservation of unrelated safety stops, no mutation of the saved encounter or vendor reference, and invalidation/translation of policy versus whole-state review fingerprints.

The adapter does not calculate renal function, assess interactions, establish tolerability/stabilization, choose a dose, prescribe an oral or injectable restart regimen, or determine clinical clearance. Historical source facts inside the unmodified vendored catalog retain their original provenance; corrected timing consumers must use this adapter. The full product instructions and active order remain necessary, including for preparation, incomplete administration, and specialist settings.
